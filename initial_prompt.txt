## Project ASTRA — Autonomous Space Telehealth & Risk Assessment

### NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026

## 1. Your Role and Mission

You are a senior full-stack software engineer, AI/ML engineer, 3D visualization developer, and UI/UX designer.

Your task is to **design, implement, integrate, test, and document a complete working software prototype** for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026.

Project name: **ASTRA — Autonomous Space Telehealth & Risk Assessment**

Challenge: **Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions**

Build a visually impressive, technically credible, offline-first astronaut health-monitoring application that combines:

- An interactive 3D astronaut health-assessment booth
- Real webcam-based astronaut identification
- Real facial landmark and historical change analysis
- Simulated medical equipment and sensor integrations
- Voice-assisted health check-ins
- Experimental biochemical odor fingerprinting
- Weekly blood sampling simulations and reminders
- Personalized, explainable health anomaly detection
- Interactive health dashboards and a human body visualization
- Health guidance and follow-up assessments
- Emergency assistance
- A simulated Earth-based Mission Control dashboard
- Local SQLite persistence

The application must be a **single coherent software product**, not separate disconnected demos.

The central user experience is:

**An astronaut enters a health-monitoring station, is identified, completes an automated assessment, and receives personalized health insights and guidance. Relevant reports are subsequently shared with Mission Control through a simulated communication system.**

Because we do not have access to physical medical sensors, the application must combine genuine software functionality with interactive hardware simulations.

### Critical implementation instruction

Do not stop after providing a plan or writing project documentation.

You are expected to create actual application files, implement functional features, run the project, fix errors, and test the workflows.

Work incrementally. First establish a working end-to-end vertical slice, then expand it into the complete application.

If this repository already contains code, inspect it before making changes. Preserve useful existing work and follow the established conventions where reasonable.

Make reasonable engineering decisions independently rather than repeatedly asking for clarification.

# 2. Core Product Philosophy

ASTRA must feel like believable software for a future deep-space mission.

The application should emphasize four core capabilities:

### A. Contactless and automated assessment

Minimize unnecessary manual input.

Use real webcam input for facial identification and facial landmark analysis. Other proposed equipment will use realistic simulated data.

### B. Personalized health intelligence

Each astronaut has their own health profile, historical observations, personal baselines, and longitudinal measurements.

Health assessments should consider changes relative to the astronaut's own baseline, not only generalized reference ranges.

### C. Actionable health guidance

The software must translate measurements into understandable observations, follow-up questions, recommended assessments, and appropriate escalation pathways.

It must not claim to diagnose medical conditions from unvalidated sensor outputs.

### D. Earth-independent operation

The software must operate locally without depending on an external database, cloud inference service, or continuous communication with Earth.

Mission Control communications will be simulated.

3. Detect the presence of a face.
4. Attempt local face matching.
5. Show the likely astronaut profile.
6. Require confirmation when matching confidence is insufficient or ambiguous.

# 11. Feature 6 — Experimental Biochemical Odor Fingerprinting

Implement a simulated electronic-nose system that tracks skin volatile organic compound (VOC) fingerprints.

This is an experimental research feature.

## Important scientific distinction

The system is not an actual hormone detector.

OpenPOM predicts odor-related molecular descriptors from molecular structure; it cannot directly infer hormone concentrations from raw electronic-nose readings.

Do not claim the software can detect cortisol, testosterone, estrogen, or progesterone levels from ordinary body odor.

Instead, demonstrate **individual VOC fingerprint change detection**.

## Implementation

Build a simulated electronic-nose sensor adapter.

Generate structured VOC feature vectors or simulated e-nose channel readings.

Track:

- Individual baseline chemical profiles
- Current fingerprint
- Changes across mission days
- Relative deviations from baseline
- Collection conditions
- Environmental factors that could affect readings

Use suitable statistical anomaly detection, with simple, explainable methods preferred initially.

Possible models:

- Robust standardized deviations
- Isolation Forest
- PCA for exploratory visualization

Clearly label any synthetic profiles.

## UI

Show:

- Animated VOC sensor activation
- Chemical fingerprint visualization
- Historical pattern comparison
- Detected deviations
- Explanations of possible measurement interference
- Recommended repeat measurement if needed

Example:

"Your current VOC fingerprint differs from your established baseline. Environmental contamination, hygiene products, physiological changes, or sensor variation may contribute. Further assessment is required before interpreting this as a health concern."

## Research integration

Design the module so published datasets or identified molecules can be integrated later.

OpenPOM integration is optional and must not block implementation.

If used, isolate it behind a dedicated molecular-description service, without treating its outputs as hormone estimates.

# 13. Feature 8 — Personalized AI Health Intelligence

This is a core technical differentiator.

Implement a health-assessment engine that maintains an individualized baseline for each astronaut.

Avoid training a separate large neural network from scratch for every astronaut.

Instead, combine:

1. A shared analysis engine
2. Per-astronaut historical baselines
3. Longitudinal change detection
4. Multivariate pattern analysis
5. Predefined safety and escalation rules

## Baselines

For each monitored metric, compute suitable statistics such as:

- Rolling median
- Historical variability
- Recent trend
- Difference from baseline
- Robust anomaly score

Use appropriate measurement contexts. For example, do not compare post-exercise heart rate directly with a resting-heart-rate baseline.

Handle missing measurements, sensor errors, insufficient history, and outliers.

Do not automatically treat a newly observed abnormal measurement as part of an updated normal baseline.

## Multimodal analysis

Correlate observations across:

- Vitals
- Sleep
- Facial features
- Subjective questionnaire responses
- VOC profiles
- Laboratory measurements
- Environmental context

Example synthetic scenario:

An astronaut's resting heart rate rises over several days, sleep duration declines, and fatigue increases.

The system recognizes the combined departure from baseline and recommends appropriate additional assessment.

Do not convert these observations directly into a disease diagnosis.

## Explainability

Every flagged observation should provide:

- The measurements involved
- The historical comparison
- Why the change was flagged
- Whether data is missing or uncertain
- Recommended next steps
- Data provenance

Clearly distinguish statistical anomalies from medical emergencies.

## Personalized health profile

Maintain a per-astronaut health profile that evolves through historical check-ins.

Store the baselines and sufficient historical data locally.

Make the analysis reproducible from stored measurements.

# 30. Final Product Vision

ASTRA should demonstrate how future astronauts could independently monitor their health during long-duration missions using a combination of:

**Computer Vision + Medical Sensor Integration + Personalized AI + Behavioral Health Monitoring + Experimental Biochemical Sensing + Local Decision Support + Delayed Earth Communication.**

The defining experience is:

**An astronaut walks into a futuristic medical station, the station recognizes them, automated instruments collect health measurements, software analyzes changes from their personal baseline, and the astronaut receives understandable guidance while Mission Control receives relevant health reports.**

Make the application feel like an actual prototype of a next-generation spacecraft medical system.

It must be visually compelling enough for a NASA Space Apps presentation, technically credible enough for engineering evaluation, and sufficiently integrated that the main workflow can be demonstrated live.
